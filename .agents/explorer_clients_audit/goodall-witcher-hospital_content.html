What is a District Fund Balance and how does it work for capital improvements?

A District Fund Balance is the accumulated surplus of revenues over expenditures that a school district maintains as a financial reserve. Texas school districts can strategically deploy fund balance dollars to finance facility improvements without issuing bonds or raising taxes. Because the energy savings from efficiency upgrades often replenish the fund balance over time, this approach allows districts to modernize infrastructure while maintaining fiscal responsibility.

How does an Energy Savings Performance Contract (ESPC) guarantee savings?

Under an ESPC, the energy services company (ESCO) conducts an investment-grade audit to calculate projected energy savings with engineering precision. The ESCO then contractually guarantees those savings — if actual utility reductions fall short of the guarantee in any measurement year, the ESCO pays the difference. This risk-transfer model means the school district is financially protected while receiving modernized infrastructure.

What is deferred maintenance and why is it a growing crisis for public schools?

Deferred maintenance refers to the practice of postponing facility repairs and equipment replacements due to budget constraints. Over time, this creates a compounding backlog — aging HVAC units consume more energy, leak refrigerant, and provide inconsistent temperatures, while deteriorating roofs and plumbing cause water damage and mold. The American Society of Civil Engineers estimates that U.S. public schools face a collective deferred maintenance backlog exceeding $85 billion. Addressing deferred maintenance through structured energy programs prevents small problems from becoming catastrophic failures.

How much energy can a Building Automation System (BAS) save a school district annually?

Industry data from the U.S. Department of Energy shows that BAS-equipped buildings typically reduce HVAC energy consumption by 10-30%. For a school district, this translates into tens of thousands of dollars in annual utility savings by automatically scheduling setbacks during nights, weekends, and holidays, and by identifying equipment faults before they become costly failures.

What is the typical lifespan and ROI of a commercial chiller system in a school?

A properly maintained commercial chiller has an expected service life of 20-30 years. Because water-cooled chillers operate at significantly higher efficiency ratings (often 0.5-0.6 kW/ton vs. 1.0+ kW/ton for air-cooled units), the energy savings alone can return the initial investment within 7-12 years, while providing superior cooling comfort for large campuses in hot climates like Texas.
